# Linux Philosophy and Context - Overview

## Introduction

Welcome to **Chapter 1: Philosophy and Context** of the Linux Foundation's **LFS101 (Introduction to Linux)** course. 

Linux is the backbone of modern computing—powering everything from smartphones (Android) and smart home devices to enterprise cloud infrastructure, the world's top 500 supercomputers, and modern web services. Understanding Linux begins not just with commands, but with its **origins, design philosophy, architecture, ecosystem, and community**.

## Key Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you will understand:
1. **How Linux emerged** as a collaboration between the GNU userland tools and the Linux kernel.
2. **The foundational design principles** that make Linux stable, flexible, and powerful.
3. **The terminology** required to read technical documentation, tutorials, and system logs.
4. **How to differentiate distributions** (Debian, Ubuntu, RHEL, Fedora, Arch, Alpine) and pick the right one for your use case.
5. **How to leverage the global community** for support, collaboration, and continuous learning.
